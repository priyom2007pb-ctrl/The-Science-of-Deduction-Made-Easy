THE SCIENCE OF DEDUCTION MADE EASY-THE METHOD

Here is the page where you will learn the exact step-by-step method of observation → analyzation → conclusion, or in short, as Sir Arthur Conan Doyle called it, DEDUCTION.

1. Observation

The first and foremost step to deduce correctly is to observe accurately. To make your observation as accurate as possible you must use all the 5 sensory organs that God gifted you – eye, ear, nose, tongue, skin.

Many amateurs in this field often limit their observation to what they see, but a true master always uses all five, as deceiving one of them is easy but deceiving all five is technically impossible.

2. Hypotheses Formation

The second step of deduction is forming as many hypotheses as possible from what you've observed.

For example – let's say that while at a bus stop you observe that a man in formal clothes is standing nearby while carrying a bag. He is constantly checking his watch, his finger joints are relatively thicker than average, and his nails are very well trimmed.

From these limited observations you can make several hypotheses such as:

  • He is running late for work.
  • He has to return home as soon as possible.
  • He either works a blue-collar or a white-collar job.
  • Many more hypotheses are possible from here, but I'm taking just these four.

3. The Process of Elimination

After forming all the possible hypotheses you must eliminate the impossible.

"When you have eliminated the impossible, whatever remains, however improbable, must be the truth."

But while practicing this method you must keep in mind that when you eliminate, it must be against some valid evidence or everything might go wrong, so be very cautious.

For example: from the previous hypotheses, if we try to eliminate the impossible, we must try collecting more data like the time, how clean or wrinkled his clothes are, etc.

Let's say the time is 5:30 PM. Then we can eliminate the hypothesis "he is running late for work" as the probability of going to work at 5:30 is very low.

Again, let's say his clothes are relatively clean and still have folding marks. Then we can eliminate the hypothesis of him working a blue-collar job.

4. Conclusion

The final part of your deduction journey will be drawing a conclusion from the hypothesis that remains.

For example: After you have eliminated the impossible, what remains is that he is probably returning from work and needs to get home as soon as possible, and he probably has a white-collar writing or typing job.

This is the simple step-by-step process that you can absolutely use to deduce correctly.

Now there are other things that you must keep in mind.

While making a deduction, never force a conclusion. Often many newbies start directly from the 4th step. First, they think of a particular conclusion and then they try to find evidence that will prove their point.

Mind you, that is nothing but satisfying your ego, and chances are it'll always go wrong.

Advanced Steps

There are a few other steps that can be squeezed into the 4-step process. They are:

Data Collection

Sometimes there are a lot of things to observe but only a few of them are truly important. That is what we call data collection.

Separating simple observation from data needs a lot of experience in the field, so when you start your journey in this field do not use it. Try getting as much evidence as possible.

Pattern Recognition

To understand what is impossible or unlikely to happen, you must first have an idea of what is possible or likely to happen. Thus, you can easily understand when something is strange in a case.

These two are advanced steps and chances are you will not need them from the very beginning.

Bonus Advice

Think in probabilities.
